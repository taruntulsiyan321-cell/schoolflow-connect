# CUET blueprints — the real paper, subject by subject

**Status: DRAFT for the owner's approval (TODO A1). Nothing uses this until it
is approved.** Written 2026-10-07.

The owner has no CUET papers to give, so the pattern was measured from the
papers themselves: every question of eleven complete NTA papers — 630 questions
in all — was read and classified by hand, one row each, in
[`cuet-blueprint-evidence.tsv`](cuet-blueprint-evidence.tsv). The bank holds no
past-year questions to analyse instead (0 rows carry an exam year or a PYQ
source).

**Sources.**
- NTA, *CUET (UG) 2026 Information Bulletin*, §2.3–2.6: 50 questions per test,
  all compulsory, 60 minutes, +5 / −1, objective MCQs; domain syllabus "as per
  NCERT".
- NTA, *CUET (UG) 2026 syllabus* for 101 English, 301 Accountancy, 305 Business
  Studies, 309 Economics, 319 Mathematics/Applied Mathematics, 501 General
  Aptitude Test (cuet.nta.nic.in/cuetug-2026-syllabus). The app's chapter lists
  match these one for one.
- The papers: the official 2024 NTA test booklets (Set A; Sets B–D are the same
  questions shuffled and were used only to confirm the counts), and one 2025
  shift per subject from NTA's response-sheet images, both as published by
  collegedunia. 2026 memory-based papers (two shifts per subject) were used only
  to confirm which forms still appear — memory papers are partial and lean
  towards memorable questions, so they set no proportions.

**What holds across years and what does not.** The *forms* are nearly fixed: in
every domain paper, 2024 and 2025, about half the paper is direct questions and
the other half is five statement sets, five match questions, five sequence
questions and two passages of five questions each. The *chapter spread* moves
from year to year — Economics most of all.

**Not measured, and said so.** Difficulty: NTA publishes no per-question
statistics and the papers carry no difficulty marks, so no difficulty mix here
is measured. A starting mix is proposed below for approval, to be replaced by
what students' answers show (TODO A4).

## How every paper is worded

A generated question must read like these, word for word where a convention
exists — this is part of "looks like the real paper".

| Form | NTA's wording |
|---|---|
| Direct question | A stem, four options numbered 1–4. Often "Which of the following…", "Identify…", "_____ is known as". |
| Statement set | Statements (A)–(D), sometimes (E), then "Choose the **correct** answer from the options given below:" with options such as "(A), (B) and (D) only". |
| Match | "Match **List-I** with **List-II**", each list four items (A)–(D) and (I)–(IV), often with a heading per list; options "(A) - (I), (B) - (II), (C) - (III), (D) - (IV)". |
| Sequence | "Arrange the following … in the correct sequence / order" (or "chronological sequence (earliest to latest)"), items (A)–(D) or (E); options are orderings "(B), (A), (D), (C)". |
| Passage | "Read the passage and answer the following questions", then five questions (six in English) on that passage. In Accountancy the passage is data and most of its questions are calculations. |

**Assertion–Reason does not appear.** Not one of the 630 questions is an
assertion–reason question, nor is one in any 2026 memory paper, although
coaching sites list the form. The bank holds 45 (Accountancy 21, Business
Studies 24). See decision 2.

## English (101) — one paper: 50 questions

|---|---:|---:|

Passages are at most 300 words (syllabus) and of three kinds — factual,
narrative, literary; 2024 had one literary/narrative and three factual. Passage
questions include meaning-in-context, title, true/not-true statements and the
author's argument. One 2026 memory paper also reports an inference question
("choose the most logical inference"); it is watched, not yet in the blueprint.

## Decisions for the owner

1. **Approve each subject's blueprint** — the form table and the chapter table
   above — or say what to change.
2. **Assertion–reason.** NTA set none in 2024, 2025 or the 2026 memory papers.
   Proposed: write no new assertion–reason questions; the 45 in the bank stay
   for practice but are never put in a mock.
3. **Economics' second half.** Proposed: every paper blends Microeconomics 12
   and Indian Economic Development 13, so a student meets both; the real paper
   has swung between them, and a blend is the honest hedge.
4. **Accountancy Unit V.** Proposed: the student chooses Analysis of Financial
   Statements or Computerised Accounting once (in their profile), and every
   paper follows that choice.
5. **Mathematics B1 or B2.** Proposed: the student chooses Mathematics or
   Applied Mathematics once, and every paper is Section A plus that section.
6. **General Knowledge and Current Affairs.** A model will confidently invent a
   "current" fact. Proposed: these questions come only from a dated, sourced
   fact list, never written freely by AI, and a current-affairs question retires
   18 months after its date.
7. **Difficulty (not measured).** Proposed starting mix: 30% easy, 50% medium,
   20% hard, by the rubric's definitions (TODO A2), replaced by measured
   difficulty once students' answers allow (TODO A4).
