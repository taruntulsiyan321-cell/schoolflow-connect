## A. Question quality — the one thing never compromised

Owner, 2026-10-04: *"The quality of the question generated by our AI shall be
top-notch. The quality of questions present in our bank shall be top-notch.
This is the only place where we can't compromise, or the student will not use
our app."* Every question a student meets must be an outstanding CBT question:
one unambiguous right answer, wrong options a student could genuinely choose,
inside the syllabus, worded as the real paper words it. **Do this section
before B** — a mock creator built on a weak bank multiplies the weakness.

**Where it stands today (measured 2026-10-03/04), honestly:**
- The CUET bank is almost all imported "PW CUET ebook" sets; 26 questions were
  written by AI Practice and 60 are recovery variants. ~95% of Accountancy and
  all of Business Studies are plain MCQ; Economics has 4 questions, English 4,
  Mathematics 2.
- An AI-written question must pass the answer check (several independent
  solves agree with its key) and the near-duplicate check (≥ 0.92 similar = the
  same question). That proves the key is consistent with the question. It does
  **not** prove the question is good: KNOWN_ISSUES 114 item 2 records case
  passages carrying data the question never uses, and loose match pairings.
- Legacy imports are partly broken: 27 match questions whose lists went into
  the options, statement and case imports with missing data (KNOWN_ISSUES 112
  item 1, 114 item 1). A disputed list exists with no owner screen (113).

**A1. The CUET pattern, derived — the owner has no CUET papers to give.**
Analyse the previous-year questions already in the bank (Practice has a PYQ
mode) and the published syllabus to state, per subject: the share of each
question type (direct MCQ, assertion–reason, statements, match the following,
sequence, case-based, numerical), how questions spread across chapters, and the
difficulty mix. Write it down as each subject's **blueprint**, show it to the
owner, and use nothing until it is approved. Feeds A2, B and C7.

**A2. A quality gate on every AI-written question, before it reaches the bank.**
A separate review pass grades each draft against a written CBT rubric and
refuses anything below the bar, recording why (`ai_practice_requests.drafts`
already records what became of each draft):
- exactly one defensible right answer; no "all/none of the above" crutches;
  no option that is right under another reading;
- every wrong option plausible — a common misconception or slip, never filler;
- every number and fact in the stem used, and nothing needed left out;
- the form done properly (assertion–reason reasons that genuinely do or do not
  explain; match lists that pair one-to-one; sequences with one order);
- inside the chapter and the syllabus; current law/standard where it applies;
- worded at the paper's level, in the paper's register; no giveaways in length
  or wording.
Done when a refused draft never reaches the bank and the refusal reasons are
measured over a batch in each subject.

**A3. Audit the existing bank against the same rubric.** Every active CUET
question, subject by subject: keep, repair (the report check's review already
rewrites a faulty question in its form) or retire. Starts with the broken
legacy match/statement/case imports and the disputed list.

**A4. Quality measured from students' answers.** Per question: right-first-time
rate, how often each wrong option is chosen, reports. Flag questions nearly
everyone gets right or wrong, and options nobody ever picks — they are not
doing work. Feeds A3 continuously.

**A5. The owner reads a sample before a subject's papers open** (B3): a page

**A6. Students' own questions** (custom uploads, screen capture): their quality

## B. The mock creator

Owner, 2026-10-03/04. A student asks for a mock — a whole subject, or one
chapter — and gets a paper that feels like the real CUET: every type of
question the real paper uses (A1's blueprint), not 50 plain MCQs.

**B1. Full subject mock:** 50 questions, 60 minutes, +5/−1 (`_mock_paper()`),
spread across the subject's chapters and types by its blueprint.

**B2. Chapter mock: also 50 questions** (owner ruling 2026-10-04). Time:
60 minutes — the paper's 72 seconds a question (my decision; say if not).

**B3. A library of saved papers, built in the background.** Writing 50 checked
questions takes minutes, so papers are prepared ahead per subject and chapter
and a student starts one at once. **A paper may be given to any number of
students; the same paper is never given to one student twice** (owner ruling).
New questions are written only for what the bank cannot supply, through A2's
gate, and saved to the bank like AI Practice's.

**B4. When a chapter runs out — DECIDED (the owner asked me to decide).** A
chapter can only hold so many genuinely different good questions (the owner's
figure: about 300). A student who takes a 7th chapter mock may have met most
of them. The rule, in order:
1. A paper is built first from the chapter's questions **this student has not
   met**.
2. When fewer than 50 are left unmet, the rest come from questions they have
   met, **the ones they got wrong or only guessed first**, then those they got
   right longest ago — a repeat that is worth repeating, which is what
   recovery and revision do anyway.
3. The paper says so before it starts: "This chapter's paper includes 18
   questions you've seen before — the ones you got wrong first."
4. The generator keeps writing new questions for the chapter only while they
   pass A2 and the near-duplicate check finds them new. When most new drafts
   are refused as duplicates, the chapter is saturated: writing stops for it
   and the owner is told, rather than paying for questions that are the same.
5. The paper as a whole is still never one the student has taken.

**B5. After the paper:** the same four-tab analysis practice sessions have
(Summary, Topics, Time, Questions — with type, guesses and marks), not today's
counts and review alone.

**B6. Supply first:** Economics, English and Mathematics cannot have a paper
until their banks are filled, through A2. Do not open their mocks on
AI-written questions that have not passed the gate.

**B7. Plan counting — to be discussed with the owner last.** Today the free
plan allows 1 mock in total and counts at start (`mock_test.start`).
